What slows the cashier down

  • The order lives behind the Cart button — you cannot see what you are charging while you pick items.
  • No quantity control anywhere. Two coffees means tapping the tile twice; three means three taps and no way back except deleting the line.
  • Two big cards per row, so a 22-item merch list is a long scroll.
  • Category is a 3-tab row, and merch families add a second filter row underneath.
  • Size is a dropdown inside each card — two taps and a menu before the item is even added.
  • Payment method is a dropdown near the bottom of the sheet; no cash-tendered field and no change due.
  • The ad-hoc discount block (three inputs) is always open in the middle of the order, above the customer section.

What changes, and why

  • Order stays on screen. The cart moves out of the slide-over and sits beside the grid, so the cashier reads the running total while they ring items up. On a phone it collapses back to a sheet.
  • Quantity steppers on every line — the explicit ask. Minus at 1 removes the line. Queue-handoff lines stay locked, exactly as they are today.
  • Dense tile grid — roughly four tiles across instead of two cards, with the running quantity badged on the tile so the cashier sees what is already in the order without looking away.
  • One category rail replaces the 3-tab row plus the merch-family toolbar. Services, Detailing and each merch family are peers.
  • Size picks inline on the tile (M / L / XL) instead of a dropdown — one tap, no menu.
  • Tenders are three big buttons, not a dropdown, and Cash opens quick-cash chips plus a change-due readout. Card and GCash skip straight to Charge.
  • Discount and customer fold away into one drawer, so the default order panel is just lines, total and Charge.
  • Charge button states the amount — “Charge ₱2,000” — so there is no silent mis-tap at the end of a shift.
